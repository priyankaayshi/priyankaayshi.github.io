Hi, I am Priyanka. I am a civil engineer and graduate researcher at the Institute of Water and Flood Management, Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET), where I am pursuing an MSc in Humanitarian Engineering (2025–present) following a BSc in Civil Engineering from BUET (2019–2024). My research centers on water resources and flood management, climate change and adaptation, remote sensing and GIS, coastal ecosystems and resilience, environmental vulnerability, and urban sustainability — most recently examining how mangrove ecosystem condition relates to household vulnerability along the Teknaf Peninsula, Cox's Bazar.

MSc Thesis Research — Fine-Scale Spatial Coupling of Mangrove Ecosystem Condition and Household Vulnerability in Sabrang Union, Teknaf Peninsula, Cox's Bazar (tentative title)

  • Investigating mangrove ecosystem condition and its relationship with coastal resilience, ecosystem services, and household vulnerability in coastal Bangladesh.
  • Using Google Earth Engine (GEE) to process Landsat and Sentinel-2 imagery and examine vegetation condition and temporal NDVI trends.
  • Applying GIS-based spatial analysis to assess mangrove distribution and spatial variations in ecosystem condition.
  • Developing a spatial assessment framework integrating ecological indicators with household-level vulnerability information.
  • Planning household surveys and semi-structured interviews to incorporate community perspectives into the assessment.

Climate-Data Analysis Using CORDEX-WAS

  • Analyzed historical and projected precipitation and temperature using CORDEX-WAS regional climate model outputs.
  • Processed NetCDF and raster-based climate datasets using R/RStudio.
  • Compared RCP4.5 and RCP8.5 scenarios to examine potential changes in precipitation and temperature.
  • Explored climate-data bias correction and historical–future climate comparisons.

Undergraduate Thesis — Permeable Block Pavements: A Sustainable Approach to Urban Flood Management and Aquifer Replenishment

  • Investigated permeable block pavements as a sustainable drainage approach for reducing surface runoff and supporting groundwater recharge.
  • Conducted experimental and field-based assessments of pavement infiltration and permeability.
  • Examined the potential of permeable pavements for urban flood mitigation and sustainable stormwater management.
